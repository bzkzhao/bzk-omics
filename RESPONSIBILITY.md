## 1. Purpose

This statement sets out which parts of `bzk-omics` are my own work and which were produced with, or by, generative AI tools. It is written to be checked rather than taken on trust: each claim below points at an artefact in the repository that a reader can inspect independently, and §7 records where no such artefact exists.

It covers the platform codebase, its documentation, and the analytical decisions recorded in the decision log. No coursework material has been produced from it to date.

## 2. Summary

I conceived the project, set its scope, made every scientific and architectural decision recorded in it, selected and validated its datasets, and verified its outputs. I did not write the majority of its source code by hand. Code was generated by an AI coding agent working from specifications I wrote or approved, executed under my direction, and audited by me against an independent clone of the repository before acceptance.

The claim I make for this work is authorship of its specification, its decisions, and its verification — not of its keystrokes.

## 4. My own contributions

**Conception and scope.** The platform concept, the decision to build local-first for researchers without enterprise licences, and the two-graph architecture separating reference entities from evidence entities. The decision that the platform sits *downstream* of Perseus and does not reimplement it — recorded in the treatment of external analyses as `Analysis.kind = 'external'` with `parameters_observed = false`.

**Scientific direction.** Selection of PXD018299 as anchor dataset; adoption of reproduction of published ISGylation targets as the regression baseline against which the platform is judged; selection of the second deposit and the reasoning that a public-survey route could not supply one.

**Domain rulings.** Every judgement in the system requiring biochemical knowledge is mine. Representative examples, each traceable to the decision log or ontology document:

- The promotion clause: promotion to a canonical accession is a *keying rule*, not an inference, and therefore travels on the observation rather than firing an assignment edge.
- Amending the affected section rather than relaxing the corresponding invariant, on the grounds that promotion is categorised as a keying rule.
- Rejecting sequence-version-in-key because anchors feed the content digest, which would make evidence-node identity a function of ingestion date and break replay determinism.
- Requiring isoform to form part of the primary key, following the finding that a substantial proportion of razor picks are isoform accessions with differing positions.
- Withdrawing my own proposed flanking-sequence alternative after checking the drift module and finding it unsupported.

**Decisions and their status.** The architectural decision record series is a record of my decisions. Four of thirty-two records remain at *Proposed* rather than *Accepted*, and known defects are carried by number in each work item's out-of-scope list. The prose of these records is model-refined; the rulings they record are mine (§5).

**Verification.** I audit all generated output against an independent clone before acceptance, and I maintain a strict linear git history with commit counts verified from the repository rather than inherited from prior reports.

**Interpretation.** The reading of the cross-dataset result — site-level modification signal separating from protein abundance signal across the published target set — is mine.

**Collaboration.** All contact with the depositing laboratory, handling of embargoed material, and the open methodological question currently blocking ingestion are mine.

## 5. AI-generated contributions

**Source code.** The majority of the codebase was generated by Claude Code from specifications I wrote or approved. This includes the dataset source modules, the Perseus spreadsheet reader (merged-cell header composition, file-type sniffing, protein-column selection), curation-record scaffolding, and the test suite. I specified behaviour, reviewed output, identified defects, and accepted or rejected each change.

**Verification tooling.** Two of the instruments this statement relies on are themselves model-written: `prompt-check.py`, which audits work items before issue, and `curriculum-facts.sh`, which regenerates the project's stated figures from a fresh clone. I commissioned and adapted both. Generated tooling checking generated code is not self-validating, and I do not present it as such: the figures `curriculum-facts.sh` produces are independently derivable from the repository by hand, and that is the check I would ask a sceptical reader to run.

**Documentation.** The repository's documents fall into three classes:

| Document | Class |
|---|---|
| `ONTOLOGY.md`, including the invariant set | Written by me; refined in language by model, not in structure or content |
| `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `OPERATIONS.md`, prose body of the decision records | Written by me; model-refined |
| `HANDOFF.md`, `REVIEWER-HANDOFF.md` | Model-written |

In the first two classes the content, structure and argument are mine and the model acted on wording. A reader wishing to test that distinction can diff any of these files against its first commit.

**Prompts and review.** Work items issued to the coding agent were drafted with AI assistance and passed through the automated checker before issue; generated output was reviewed with AI assistance before my own audit. Both the checker's findings and the review's findings were verified by me, not accepted. See §7 on the evidential limits of this claim.

## 6. Where the boundary was drawn

Several results are neither purely mine nor purely generated, and I set them out rather than assign them:

- **Empirical findings that changed the schema** — for example the proportion of filtered modification sites mapping to more than one protein, which invalidated a cardinality assumption. The measurement was executed by machine; the question was mine; the schema consequence was my ruling.
- **Defects.** Defects were surfaced by both routes — automated checking and my own audit. Discovery source was not recorded at the time and resolved defects were removed from the log rather than archived, so the split between the two cannot be substantiated and I do not state one (§7).
- **Imputation as load-bearing.** That imputation moved reproduction from one to twelve of fourteen published targets is a measured result. Recognising it as a claim requiring disclosure rather than a tuning detail was my decision.

## 7. How a reader can check this — and where they cannot

Checkable in the repository:

- Git history: linear, no merges; decisions embedded in commit messages.
- The decision record series, including records still at *Proposed*.
- The current defect log, carried by number.
- `ONTOLOGY.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `OPERATIONS.md` and the decision records across their commit history, for the authorship classes given in §5.
- `curriculum-facts.sh`, whose output is reproducible by hand against the repository.

Not checkable:

- Prompt and checker records are not retained. The claims in §5 about how work items were drafted, audited before issue, and verified after return are therefore my account, unsupported by artefact. They are the least evidenced claims in this statement and should be read as such.
- Defect history is incomplete. Resolved defects were removed from the log rather than archived, and discovery source was never recorded, so neither the total number of defects raised over the project nor the division between automated and manual discovery can be recovered.

## 8. Limitations of this statement

This statement describes a division of responsibility at the level of decision, specification and verification. It does not claim line-by-line attribution of the codebase, and such a claim could not be substantiated honestly. Where the boundary is genuinely unclear, or where no evidence survives, I have said so above rather than resolve it in my own favour.
